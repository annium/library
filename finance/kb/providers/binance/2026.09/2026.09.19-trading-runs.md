# Three trading runs, and what the first one cost to get there — 2026-09-19

The write block run end to end against the live USD-M futures account, three times, with the full trace
of every run kept and read before the next one started. The three clean runs are the last three; what
came before them is the more useful half of this document, because each failure was a real defect and
none of them looked like one at first.

## The three runs

Same binary, same account, each started from a flat account and a rested weight budget.

| run | result | duration | requests | peak weight | connector errors |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 of 8 | 88s | 163 | 1132 | 0 |
| 2 | 8 of 8 | 80s | 162 | 948 | 0 |
| 3 | 8 of 8 | 81s | 163 | 1278 | 0 |

They did the same work each time, which is the part worth asserting: 8 ordinary orders, 6 conditional
orders, the same mix of limit, market, stop and take-profit, and the same count of cancel-alls. The only
figure that moved between runs was the number of listen-key keepalives (4 or 5), which follows the clock
rather than the test.

The account was left flat after each: no position, no open order, no conditional order.

## What the failures were

### A connect that timed out disabled the socket for good

The first run failed two tests. One sat for five minutes and died on its deadline; the other reported
`NotConnected` while the exchange was answering normally. Both came from one line in
`Annium.Net.WebSockets`: a single cancellation source served as both the deadline for one handshake and
the answer to "does anybody still want this socket". A handshake that overran five seconds cancelled that
source, and the reconnect it triggered then waited out its delay on the very token that had just been
cancelled — so the retry the timeout exists to produce was the one thing the timeout prevented. The
cancellation was swallowed as an ordinary shutdown, and the socket stayed `Connecting` silently forever.

The fix separates the two meanings: the lifetime source is cancelled by `Disconnect` and `Dispose` and
nothing else, and every attempt gets a linked source carrying its own deadline. The test drives it from a
listener that accepts the TCP connection and answers nothing, so the handshake can end no way other than
on its own deadline; nothing connects and nothing is meant to, and the assertion is that a second attempt
happens at all.

Worth keeping: the hung test was not idle while it hung. Its connector went on polling once a second for
five minutes, and that is what exhausted the account's weight budget and failed the *other* test. A hang
in one place bills the next thing along.

### The block was living at 83% of the venue's allowance

Measured one endpoint at a time, alone, on a rested budget:

| endpoint | weight |
|---|---|
| server time | 1 |
| instrument list | 1 |
| account | 5 |
| open orders, unscoped | 40 |
| open orders, scoped to one symbol | 1 |
| conditional orders | 40 |

The conditional-order list costs exactly what the unscoped order list does, so adding it to the
reconciliation path took a cycle from 45 to 85. With this suite reloading orders every second, that is
4800 against an allowance of 2400 a minute; the block ran at 2000, and two runs in a row could not both
fit. The second one failed seven of eight tests, every one of them the connector declaring itself not
connected while the exchange was fine.

This is the suite's configuration, not the connector's: production reloads orders once a minute. Raised
to fifteen seconds here, the peak fell to ~1100 and the run got no slower — which says the tests were
being driven by the user stream all along, and the poll was only a fallback they never needed.

Arithmetic over a live run cannot produce this table. Requests overlap, so a rise in the counter cannot
be attributed to whichever request finished first: the naive attribution charged 218 weight to an
endpoint that costs 1.

### Two assertions that were wrong about a healthy connector

Both surfaced only once the socket started retrying instead of hanging.

Connector errors were counted from before the connector was ready. Reaching a live venue is allowed to
take more than one attempt — a handshake over the public internet sometimes overruns the socket's
five-second deadline — and each failed attempt raises an error the socket then recovers from. A run was
failed by two of those, ten seconds before the connector came up healthy and did everything asked of it.
They are counted from readiness now.

And the assertion named nothing: "expected to be empty, but has 4 items", against a log of tens of
thousands of lines. It lists the messages now.

## The two things to carry to the next venue

**A live suite is not repeatable unless it fits the budget with room to spare.** Not "under the limit" —
a run at 83% cannot be followed by another, and the failure it produces names connectivity rather than
rate limits, so it reads as a broken connector.

**A probe is not a test.** Four of them sat in the read and write blocks, trading to answer questions and
overwriting the captures they had written last time. They have a block of their own now, which no recipe
runs.
