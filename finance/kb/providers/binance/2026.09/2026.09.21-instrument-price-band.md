# Run report — the price band reaches the instrument model, both market types

> The gap §11 named on the morning of the same day: a live order refused by a filter the provider read
> and discarded, leaving the caller with a refusal that names a rule instead of a bound.

## What was missing

`InstrumentModel` carried the price, lot, notional and order-count limits. It did not carry the band a
limit order's price has to sit in relative to the venue's own reference price — the filter that refused
three prices during the spot trading block's first live run. A caller pricing away from the market could
only discover the bound by being refused.

## The two spellings

They diverge in more than field names, which is the part worth carrying:

| | spot | usd-futures |
|---|---|---|
| filter | `PERCENT_PRICE_BY_SIDE` | `PERCENT_PRICE` |
| reference | average of recent trades over `avgPriceMins` | mark price |
| what is stated | both ends, per side (four multipliers) | one inequality per side (two multipliers) |
| what that leaves | a closed band on each side | a buy with no floor, a sell with no ceiling |

The second row of that table is why the model stores *ratios* and not prices: the reference is the
venue's, computed on its side, and a caller substituting the current bid or ask is approximating. The
fourth row is why the model stores four ends and not two.

## The mistake this was written to avoid

Reading the futures pair as a two-sided band is the obvious simplification, and it is wrong in the
expensive direction: it makes the provider refuse orders the exchange accepts. It is also refuted by
evidence already in hand rather than by argument — the futures trading block rests buys at 0.7 of the
market while `DOTUSDT` publishes `multiplierDown: 0.9500`, and all three clean runs went through. A
symmetric reading would have refused every one of them locally.

## Census, taken live

Both public `exchangeInfo` payloads, 2026-09-21. Every symbol open for trading carries the band: spot
1368 of 1368, futures 773 of 773. Spot's window is `avgPriceMins: 5` on all 1368 and no spot symbol
carries the older `PERCENT_PRICE`. The values are not uniform — spot's most common quadruple covers 898
symbols of 1368, futures' most common pair 426 of 773 — so nothing about a default can be assumed.

The census is what settled a design question rather than decorating one: universal today, so it *could*
have been required, and a required filter whose absence drops the whole symbol is what the other four
do. It is read as optional instead, because an absent band is representable as an unbounded one, where
an absent price or lot filter leaves nothing to compute an order from. The day a symbol arrives without
one, it stays tradable rather than silently disappearing.

## What changed

- `IInstrument` and `InstrumentModel` gained four ratios, with zero keeping its established meaning:
  the provider does not bound that end. The remark on the interface says where the reference price comes
  from and that the band need not be symmetric.
- `InstrumentExtensions` gained `MinSidePrice`, `MaxSidePrice` and `IsValidSidePrice`, which read the
  end belonging to the side an order would be placed on.
- Both venue filter converters read their own filter into one `PercentPriceFilter`, written in the
  model's vocabulary because the wire's has no shared spelling.

## What the tests pin, and what a mutation proved

Both venues gained the `InstrumentFiltersConverterTests` file they never had — a standing backlog item,
closed here because the band is exactly the filter a single captured `exchangeInfo` payload cannot
interrogate. Driving the filters array directly makes a case a dozen lines instead of a hundred, which
is what makes it affordable to ask for a band whose four ends are four different numbers, and for an
array without one.

Seven mutants were run against the new assertions. Six died: sides transposed, the futures band widened
to two-sided, an absent band defaulting to one, a side price reading the wrong end (twice), and an
unstated ceiling read literally.

The seventh survived, and was worth more than the six: guarding the *lower* end with `> 0` is dead
logic. An unstated floor reads as zero and no price is below zero, so the guard can never fire — it was
written out of symmetry with the upper end, where the same zero does need guarding or every price would
be refused. The guard was removed and the asymmetry explained in place; two further mutants against the
rewritten form both died.
