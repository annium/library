# Run report — the first fills on spot, and what they settled

> The last thing this market type had never done. Every order its trading block places rests far from
> the market by design, so the events an execution raises were documented, transcribed, pinned — and
> never once seen.

## What was run

Two real round trips on `DOTUSDT`, each about 7 USDT, approved as their own stage and run one at a
time: a market buy with the market sell that returned it, then a limit buy priced 0.5% through the ask
with the limit sell that returned it. A probe, not a test — it records, and asserts only that the venue
filled what it was asked to fill.

Before either, the account was read with the standing cleanup tool: 34.15 USDT free, nothing open on
the symbol, no position. After both it holds 34.11 USDT and 0.00415 DOT of dust — the fee on a buy is
taken in the asset bought, and what remains of it is below the lot step.

Every answer is in [`2026.09.21-spot-fill/`](2026.09.21-spot-fill/README.md), with a README saying what
each file is.

## What the bytes settled

Six facts, all now `live` in §11 of the manifest. Three of them were already implemented correctly and
are worth as much as the ones that were not — a transcription confirmed against bytes is the only kind
that stops being a reading:

- **A fill is its own event.** Acceptance and execution arrive as two `executionReport`s, and the
  second is the one carrying the trade.
- **The acceptance event carries a null commission asset** and an amount spelled `"0"` rather than
  padded. This is the one real gap the capture found: every fixture in the suite had spelled both
  otherwise, because every fixture had been assembled from the documentation.
- **A marketable limit fills at the book's price** — placed at 1.202, filled at 1.197. The payload that
  distinguishes a converter computing the execution price from what it cost from one reading the
  order's own price; a resting order's fixtures cannot tell them apart.
- **The fee's asset depends on the side**, which is why returning a bought quantity means subtracting
  the fee and re-aligning to the lot step. The probe does exactly that, and had to.
- **`outboundAccountPosition` is a delta**, carrying three assets on an account holding five. Harmless
  here, because the connector reads none of its balances and treats the event as a signal to reload —
  but recorded, because anything publishing from it directly would erase what it does not mention.
- **The stream event and the placement's `fills` agree**, so a trade has two possible sources and the
  connector deliberately uses a third: it asks for the symbol's trades, so a fill reaches a caller once.

## What was pinned afterwards

Two tests in `OrderUpdateEventConverterTests`, both written from the captured payloads verbatim: the
acceptance event with its null commission asset, and the crossing limit's fill with its price
improvement. Three mutants run against them, all dead — the execution price read from the order's
price, read from the last fill rather than the cumulative quotient, and the null commission asset kept
as null.

The first two mutants had to be written to keep every variable used: deleting the use of the cumulative
sum makes it unused, the build fails on analysers, and a runner given `--no-build` then reports the
previous binary green. That is the failure mode the skill warns about, met twice in one pass.

## What this does not settle

Whether the executed price is the average across several fills or the last one. Both round trips filled
in a single trade, where the two coincide. It is pinned offline instead, by a fixture whose cumulative
and last prices differ — which is the right place for it, since producing a partial fill on demand
means resting an order and waiting for the market.

## Verification

- offline: 596 passed, 0 failed — two of them new
- live: two probe runs, each green, account read before and after
- cost: 0.04 USDT in fees, and 0.00415 DOT of dust
