# Binance spot — step 5: the trading block, built and not run

**Date:** 2026-09-21. **Branch:** `feature/binance-spot-assessment`. **Contract:** `manifest.md` §11.

The report is immutable: it describes a run, not the current state. The living state is in `status.md`.
It follows [the offline half](2026.09.21-step-5-spot.md), which said this did not exist.

**Nothing here was executed against the exchange.** The block is written, wired and excluded from every
routine run; running it is its own gate and has not been asked for.

## The finding that decided the shape of it

The shared trading fixture **cannot be used on a cash account**, and not for a reason that shows up as a
wrong assertion. It is built around positions existing:

- it waits for a position snapshot before it will start, and a cash account reports none — ever. The wait
  does not fail, it does not end;
- it checks the account's position mode, and throws when no position reports the range it expects;
- its cleanup closes what it finds.

The first two would have produced a fixture that hangs to its deadline. The third is the one worth
stating plainly: **on a margined account, flattening is restorative** — it returns the account to where it
started. **On a cash account the balances are the holdings**, so the same instinct sells whatever the
account owns. There is no state on a cash account that a test fixture is entitled to undo except the
orders it placed itself.

So the trading block gets a fixture of its own, whose cleanup cancels orders and nothing else.

## The block trades nothing

Every order it places is a **limit buy at half the market**, which rests until it is cancelled. That
exercises placement, the account stream reporting it, the balance locking behind it, replacement and
cancellation — and buys nothing, sells nothing and pays no fee, because an unfilled order has no fee.

The prices and sizes are read from the instrument rather than written down: the resting price is half the
current bid rounded down to the tick and clamped to the instrument's minimum, and the quantity comes from
the instrument's own minimum notional with a margin over it, so a rounding that goes the wrong way does
not turn the test into a filter refusal.

**What this leaves unasserted live, stated rather than hidden:** a fill, and everything downstream of one
— the trade the reload publishes, the commission on it, a partially filled order's arithmetic. Those are
pinned offline against recorded payloads. Making them live means buying something, which costs money and
changes holdings, and that is a decision rather than a detail.

## What was written

| stage | block | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `UserConnectorReadTests` | `read` | connects and reads; places nothing. The cheapest test that can tell the new transport works at all — reaching connected means the socket opened, a signed subscription was accepted and the loaders reported in |
| `UserConnectorTests` | `write` | four cases: a resting order placed, reported, locking and releasing balance; a replacement that is a new order; a refusal below the minimum notional; cancel-all clearing a resting order |
| `AccountCleanupTool` | `probe` | cancels every open order on the symbol and reports what it found. Run by hand, by no recipe |

The cleanup tool exists **before** the first trading run rather than after it, which is the rule and the
reason for it: a stage that fails part-way does not clean up after itself — teardown runs at the end of a
passing test, and an assertion that throws or a runner that cancels the job leaves whatever the stage
placed on a real account. Writing the tool then means writing it while something has just gone wrong and
it is not yet clear what.
